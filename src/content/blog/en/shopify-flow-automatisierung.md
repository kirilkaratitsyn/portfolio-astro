"Every morning I go through the same orders, tag customers by hand and check what's sold out." Shopify Flow can take over routines like these. The app watches for events in your store and then runs the steps you've set, without code.

## A note before we start

Everything here comes from the Shopify Help Center as of October 2026. Menus change, so check your own admin. Menu names come from the English interface. I don't quote prices.

The Help Center doesn't say which workflows are worth it for your store, how much time they save or whether they change your sales. Triggers and actions from other apps follow that provider's rules.

## What Shopify Flow is

According to the Help Center, Shopify Flow automates tasks in your store and across your apps. The **free app** is available on the Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus plans. You install it from the Shopify App Store and open it under **Apps > Flow**.

Almost all features are available on all of these plans. The exceptions the Help Center names: **Send HTTP request** is available from Grow up, and tasks from custom partner apps only on Plus. Usage limits follow the API limits of the plan.

## The three building blocks of a workflow

In Flow, an automation is called a **workflow** and has three parts:

- **Trigger:** the event that starts the workflow, from Flow or from an app. Examples: **Order created**, **Customer created**, **Product created**, **Product variant inventory quantity changed** and **Scheduled time** for workflows that run on a schedule.
- **Condition:** checks whether the workflow continues, like an "if, then". Without a condition, the actions run right away.
- **Action:** changes something in your store, works with data, or does something in another app. Examples: **Add order tags**, **Add customer tags**, **Hold fulfillment order**, **Cancel order**, **Send internal email** and **Wait**.

According to the Help Center, workflows run as soon as possible after the trigger.

## What merchants typically use it for

Examples from the Help Center:

- **Risk:** cancel high-risk orders and restock the items, or capture payment only if the order isn't high-risk.
- **Inventory:** get notified when stock is low, hide sold-out products. See [Managing Shopify inventory](/blog/shopify-lagerbestand-verwalten).
- **Customers:** group customers into tiers by lifetime spend, or tag them by email address, for example for an education discount.
- **Orders:** tag orders paid with a gift card, or email the logistics team about urgent orders. See [Shopify gift cards and vouchers](/blog/shopify-geschenkkarten-gutscheine).
- **Fulfillment:** request or hold fulfillments based on criteria, flag unusually large quantities, tag orders by shipping method. See [Setting up Shopify shipping](/blog/shopify-versand-einrichten).
- **Payment:** send a payment reminder after the due date.

## Template or your own workflow

Under **Create workflow** you pick a template via **Browse templates** or build the workflow yourself: choose a trigger, add a condition with **+**, set the action on the **True** branch. It only goes live with **Turn on workflow**.

Notes from the Help Center:

- Templates contain placeholders. Replace them before you turn the workflow on.
- Many templates use triggers or actions from other apps. These need the matching connector installed.
- A workflow generated by Sidekick starts out inactive. Review and test it thoroughly.

## Conditions: what often surprises people

- **AND and OR:** with AND every criterion must be true, with OR one is enough.
- **Missing data:** with no data, AND counts as **true** and OR as **false**, according to the Help Center. That is why some workflows fire too often or never.
- **Lists:** for lists, such as line items, there are "at least one of", "all of" and "none of". If the list is empty, nothing is evaluated.
- **Negation:** the Help Center calls reversing a condition with "is not equal to" a common mistake.
- **Upper and lower case** don't matter in text comparisons.
- **Tags:** with more than 250 tags on an order, product or customer, tag conditions might not work.
- **Orders and drafts** are separate. Triggers and actions for one don't affect the other.

The Help Center advises a more specific trigger than **Order created**, for example **Order risk analyzed** for risk workflows.

## Waiting instead of acting right away

With **Wait**, a workflow pauses, for example to check an order later. According to the Help Center:

- At most **90 days** per workflow, otherwise it can't be turned on. For longer gaps, the Help Center recommends a scheduled workflow.
- After the wait, the data is **refreshed**. The current state is what counts.
- If you edit an active workflow, the change also applies to waiting runs. Turning it off or deleting it stops them.

## Test before it goes live

You test a workflow before turning it on via **Test your workflow** at the trigger. According to the Help Center:

- The test uses real store data but doesn't change your store.
- It stops at the first action that would make a change.
- Actions that reach external services, such as Send HTTP request, can't be simulated.
- Tests don't appear in the list of runs.

## When something goes wrong

- All runs are under **Apps > Flow > Recent runs**, with the statuses **In progress**, **Waiting** or **Completed** and filters, for example for errors.
- Runs are stored for **14 days** after they complete.
- Flow retries temporary errors with growing gaps, for at most 36 hours per section of a workflow. Flow doesn't retry permanent errors, and the run is marked as failed.
- You can retry failed runs by hand.
- The **Workflow error occurred** trigger reports errors via a Shopify alert, email or Slack.

## The "Send internal email" action

According to the Help Center, it's meant for emails to **your own team**. For customers, the Help Center points to marketing automations. The recipient address is fixed and can't use variables. Separate several recipients with a comma. What Shopify itself sends to customers: [Shopify email notifications](/blog/shopify-email-benachrichtigungen).

## When a developer helps

You can set up simple workflows from templates yourself. Help is worth it when:

- a workflow needs data from metafields, see [Shopify metafields and metaobjects](/blog/shopify-metafelder-metaobjects),
- Flow should talk to external systems via HTTP or the Admin API,
- a workflow connects several apps,
- wholesale customers should be approved automatically, as with [Shopify B2B and wholesale](/services/shopify-b2b).

After that, the workflows belong to ongoing care, see [Shopify maintenance and support](/blog/shopify-wartung-betreuung).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Shopify Flow cost anything?** According to the Help Center, Flow is a free app on the Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus plans.

**Can Flow send emails to customers?** Send internal email is meant for your own team. For customers, the Help Center points to marketing automations.

**Does a test change my store?** No. According to the Help Center, it stops before the first action that would make a change.

**How long can a workflow wait?** At most 90 days.

**Where can I see whether a workflow ran?** Under Apps > Flow > Recent runs, for 14 days after the run completes.

**What does the setup cost?** That depends on the scope. I don't quote amounts here.
