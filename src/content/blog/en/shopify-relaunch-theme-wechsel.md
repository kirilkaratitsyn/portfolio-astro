If you want to hire someone for a Shopify relaunch, your store probably looks dated, is hard to navigate or works worse on a phone than on a desktop. The worry: the store sells every day. What happens to orders, products and Google addresses during the rebuild?

## Three things "relaunch" can mean

- **Tidying up the existing theme.** Shorten sections, remove apps, reorder pages. This is often enough if the theme fits: [Hiring someone to customize your Shopify theme](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).
- **Switching themes.** A new theme with a new structure for the home page, collections and product page. Products, customers and orders stay in Shopify.
- **Switching platforms.** From WooCommerce, Wix or Shopware to Shopify. A different job with its own risks, such as redirects and data transfer: [WooCommerce to Shopify](/blog/woocommerce-to-shopify) and [Shopify migration and SEO](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen).

If the store just "looks outdated", first work out what exactly bothers you.

## Structure before design

The most common mistake: pick a theme first, plan the pages afterwards. The reverse works better:

1. Which pages does the store need (home page, collections, product page, information pages)?
2. Which sections are on each page, in which order?
3. What data fills these sections: fixed text in the theme, or product data from [metafields](/blog/shopify-metafelder-metaobjects) that your team maintains in Shopify?

Ask to see the structure before the build, as a sketch of each page. The theme is chosen only after you approve it.

## How a theme switch works in Shopify

According to the Shopify Help Center:

- A store can have up to 20 themes, but only one is published. That is the one your customers see.
- All the others sit under "Draft themes". The new theme is built there, and visitors do not see it.
- When you publish, the previous theme moves to the drafts. No changes are lost, and you can switch back.
- Before changes, Shopify recommends making a copy ("Duplicate").

So the relaunch is built next to the live store and goes live only after your approval.

## What does not carry over automatically

- **Settings and customizations.** What was built in the old theme with a setting or code does not automatically exist in the new one. Ask for a list: what can the new theme do out of the box, what gets rebuilt, what goes away?
- **Apps.** Some apps integrate into the theme. Every integration is checked during the switch. This also shows which app the new theme makes unnecessary.
- **Content outside the theme.** According to the Help Center, a theme download does not include products, collections, menus, pages, blog posts or files. They stay in the store. A duplicate backs up the theme, not the store.

## The theme: license and source

From the Shopify Help Center:

- A theme from the Shopify Theme Store is licensed exclusively to the store it was bought for. Unlicensed themes get neither support nor updates. According to Shopify, an uploaded theme that is identified as unlicensed can be neither customized nor published until the license is resolved.
- Shopify advises against themes from sources other than the Theme Store, because they are not vetted for quality and compatibility.
- Older "Vintage" themes do not support everything newer ones do. Shopify's free Vintage themes only receive security updates. The theme editor shows whether your theme is one of them.

## Google: addresses and page titles

With a pure theme switch, the addresses of products and collections stay the same. Where pages disappear or addresses change, you need redirects: [Shopify migration and SEO: setting up redirects](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen).

## How to spot a good offer

- **Scope**: tidy-up, theme switch or platform switch, and which pages are included.
- **Structure before build**: a sketch of each page that you approve.
- **Preview instead of live work**: built in an unpublished theme, published after your written go-ahead.
- **A phone checklist**: from the home page to checkout, with a test order.
- **Apps, addresses and license** are settled: which theme, from where, on whose license.
- **Access without a password**: through an access request ("collaborator") or a staff account of its own, not through your login.
- **A fixed price after the briefing**, with the scope defined. Anyone who names a sum without talking to you does not know your store.
- **After go-live**: a window for corrections and who looks after the store afterwards, see [Shopify maintenance and support](/blog/shopify-wartung-betreuung).

Nobody can honestly guarantee that a new design sells more or ranks higher. What can be promised is a clearer structure, a store that works on a phone as it does on a desktop, and a switch without a break in sales.

## Go-live: how to proceed

1. Go through the preview together, on a phone and on a desktop.
2. Run a test order, including a discount code and shipping.
3. Give your approval in writing.
4. Publish. The old theme stays as a draft.
5. The same day, check the home page, a collection, a product, the cart and checkout on a phone. Afterwards, look at the Search Console for new errors.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does my store keep running during the relaunch?** Yes. The new theme is built as an unpublished preview.

**Will I lose products, customers or orders?** No. They live in Shopify, not in the theme.

**Can I go back if I do not like the new theme?** Yes. According to the Help Center, the previous theme then sits among the drafts.

**Do I need a new theme at all?** Not always. Often a rework of the existing one is enough, and the briefing settles that.

**What does a relaunch cost?** It depends on the number of pages, the range and the state of the data. After the briefing you get a fixed-price offer.
