If you are looking for someone to relaunch your Shopify store, you usually have a store that looks dated, has become hard to navigate or works worse on a phone than on a desktop. The worry behind it is almost always the same: the store sells every day. What happens to orders, products and Google addresses when someone rebuilds it? This article shows what a relaunch can cover, how a theme switch works in Shopify, what does not carry over automatically and how to spot a good offer.

## Three things "relaunch" can mean

- **Tidying up the existing theme.** Shorten sections, remove apps, reorder pages. This is often enough if the theme basically fits. What you can do yourself and when code is needed is covered in [Hiring someone to customize your Shopify theme](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).
- **Switching themes.** A new theme with a new structure for the home page, collections and product page. Products, customers and orders stay in Shopify; the switch changes how the store looks.
- **Switching platforms.** From WooCommerce, Wix or Shopware to Shopify. That is a different job with its own risks, such as redirects and data transfer: [WooCommerce to Shopify](/blog/woocommerce-to-shopify) and [Shopify migration and SEO](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen).

A good offer says which of the three is meant. If all you have is a feeling that the store "looks outdated", the work starts with the question of what exactly bothers you, not with a theme.

## Structure before design

The most common mistake in a relaunch: you pick a pretty theme first and only then think about which pages the store needs. The reverse order works better:

1. Which pages does the store really need (home page, collections, product page, information pages)?
2. Which sections are on each page, in which order, and why?
3. What data fills these sections: fixed text in the theme, or product data from [metafields](/blog/shopify-metafelder-metaobjects) that your team maintains in Shopify?

Only with this structure can you choose a theme sensibly. Ask to see the structure before anything is built, as a sketch of each page that you approve.

## How a theme switch works in Shopify

The mechanics are simpler than many think, and they are the reason the live store can keep selling. According to the Shopify Help Center:

- You can have up to 20 themes in your store, but only one is published. The published theme is the one your customers see.
- All the others sit under "Draft themes". The new theme can be built and previewed there without visitors seeing it.
- When you publish a new theme, the previous one moves to the draft themes. None of your theme changes are lost. Switching back is possible.
- Before you change a theme, Shopify recommends making a copy ("Duplicate") so you can start over if needed.

So the relaunch is built next to the live store. Only the click on "Publish" swaps the theme, and that should happen only after your approval.

## What does not carry over automatically

A theme switch is not a move at the push of a button. Three points should be clear beforehand:

- **Settings and customizations.** Every theme has its own settings and sections. What was built in the old theme with a setting or code does not automatically exist in the new one. Ask for a list: what can the new theme do out of the box, what has to be rebuilt, what goes away?
- **Apps.** Some apps integrate into the theme. Every integration has to be checked during the switch. It is also the moment to clarify which app the new theme makes unnecessary.
- **Content outside the theme.** According to the Help Center, a theme download does not include products, collections, menus, pages, blog posts or files. They stay in the store, and a theme switch does not touch them. A duplicate of the theme is a backup of the theme, not of the whole store.

## The theme: license and source

Two points from the Shopify Help Center that you should raise in the conversation:

- A theme bought in the Shopify Theme Store is licensed exclusively to the store it was bought for. Unlicensed themes get no support and no updates. According to Shopify, an uploaded theme that is identified as unlicensed can be neither customized nor published until the license question is resolved.
- Shopify advises against installing themes from sites other than the Theme Store, because they are not vetted for quality and compatibility.
- Themes come in different architecture versions. Older "Vintage" themes do not support everything newer ones do, and Shopify's free Vintage themes only receive security updates. You can see whether your current theme is one of them in the theme editor.

So ask in the offer: which theme, from where, on whose license?

## Google: addresses and page titles

With a pure theme switch, the addresses of products and collections stay the same. Where pages disappear or addresses change, you need redirects, otherwise visitors and Google end up on dead pages. How to do that cleanly is covered in [Shopify migration and SEO: setting up redirects](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen). After go-live, also check the Search Console for new errors.

## How to spot a good offer

- **It names the scope**: tidy-up, theme switch or platform switch, and which pages are included.
- **Structure before build**: a sketch of each page that you approve before anything is built.
- **Preview instead of live work**: it is built in an unpublished theme, your store keeps selling.
- **Approval before go-live**: the theme is published only after your written go-ahead.
- **A phone checklist**: home page, collection, product page, cart and checkout tested on a phone, with a test order.
- **A clear approach to apps, addresses and license**, as described above.
- **Access without a password**: through an access request ("collaborator") or a staff account of its own, not through your login.
- **A fixed price after the briefing**, with the scope defined. Anyone who names a sum without talking to you does not know your store.
- **What happens after go-live**: a window for corrections and an answer to who looks after the store afterwards, see [Shopify maintenance and support](/blog/shopify-wartung-betreuung).

No serious provider guarantees that a new design sells more or ranks higher. What can be promised: a clearer structure, a store that works on a phone as well as on a desktop, and a switch without a break in sales.

## Go-live: how to proceed

1. Go through the preview together, on a phone and on a desktop.
2. Run a test order, including a discount code and shipping.
3. Give your approval in writing.
4. Publish. The old theme stays as a draft.
5. The same day: check the home page, a collection, a product, the cart and checkout on a phone. In the days after, look at the Search Console for new errors.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does my store keep running during the relaunch?** Yes. The new theme is built as an unpublished preview. The live store stays as it is until you approve.

**Will I lose products, customers or orders?** No. They live in Shopify, not in the theme.

**Can I go back if I do not like the new theme?** Yes. After publishing, the previous theme sits among the drafts, and according to the Help Center no changes are lost.

**Do I need a new theme at all?** Not always. Often a rework of the existing one is enough. The briefing settles that.

**What does a relaunch cost?** It depends on the number of pages, the range and the state of the data. With me you get a fixed-price offer after the briefing.
