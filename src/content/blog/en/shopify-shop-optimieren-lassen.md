If you want to hire someone to optimize your Shopify store, you usually mean: faster, easier to find or selling more. But "optimize" is a catch-all word. Without a goal and a baseline, nobody can prove the work achieved anything.

## What "optimize" can mean

Four areas should be kept apart:

- **Load time.** Apps, scripts, images and the theme. Causes and your own checks: [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).
- **Technical findability.** Errors in Search Console, sitemap, redirects, titles and descriptions. If your store does not show up on Google at all, start with [Shopify Store Not Found on Google](/blog/shopify-shop-bei-google-nicht-gefunden).
- **Usability.** Product page, cart and checkout on a phone. That is a check with test orders, not a promise of a specific increase in sales.
- **Housekeeping in the background.** Tracking scripts, leftovers of old apps, duplicate code. It saves load time and makes later changes cheaper. On scripts, pixels and consent: [Shopify cookie banner and privacy policy](/blog/shopify-cookie-banner-datenschutz).

A serious offer says which areas it covers and which it does not.

## Where to start

Write down three answers. They are the core of the brief.

1. **What bothers you, specifically?** "The store feels slow on a phone" is a different job from "customers drop out at the cart".
2. **How will you notice it got better?** A number, an error message that disappears, a test order without a hitch.
3. **What must not change?** The look, the addresses, the running campaigns.

## Before: record baseline values and a goal

Google defines three metrics, the Core Web Vitals. According to [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) these count as "good":

- **LCP** (load time of the largest element): 2.5 seconds or less
- **INP** (response to input): 200 milliseconds or less
- **CLS** (layout shift): 0.1 or less

Each is assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads, split by mobile and desktop. A page passes only with all three values.

Here is how to record the starting point:

- Measure the home page, one collection and one product page in [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/), mobile view. Write the values down.
- In Search Console, look at the Core Web Vitals report if your store has enough visitors.
- In Shopify, look at the web performance reports. According to Shopify they are based on real visitor data from the past 30 days and can be delayed by up to 36 hours.
- Save the Search Console errors as a screenshot.

"Faster" is not a goal. "The three metrics in the green range on mobile for these three page types" is one.

## What a good offer contains

These points should be in writing, with a freelancer or an agency:

- **Scope.** One sentence per area: what is changed and what is not.
- **Target values and how they are measured.** Which pages, which tool, which day.
- **Work in an unpublished theme.** The live store keeps selling.
- **Approval before go-live.** Publishing happens only after your written confirmation.
- **A list of changes.**
- **A re-measurement** with the same pages and the same tool.
- **A clear price or clear pricing logic.** With me it is a fixed price after the briefing. What the price depends on: [Having a Shopify store built: what the cost depends on](/blog/shopify-shop-erstellen-lassen-kosten).

General questions for any provider are in [Shopify freelancer or agency: six questions](/blog/shopify-freelancer-oder-agentur).

## Warning signs

- **A guaranteed first place.** On its page "Do you need an SEO?" Google says in essence: nobody can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google.
- **A score as a promise.** Lab test values vary with page, device and test run. Target ranges and a before-and-after comparison under equal conditions make sense.
- **Changes without explanation.** Google lists as a question for an SEO provider whether they will share all the changes they make to your site with you.
- **More access than needed.** See the next section.

## Giving access safely

You do not have to hand over your password. According to the Shopify help, access for developers runs through collaborator requests:

- The developer sends a request through a Partner account. You see it in the settings under "Users" and review the permissions asked for. Shopify advises granting only the permissions the task needs.
- In the Security section you can generate a four-digit code that the developer has to enter with their request. A new code invalidates old ones.
- You can remove access at any time. It expires automatically if the developer has not logged in for 90 days.

Never let anyone work directly on the live theme. Under "Online Store" you can duplicate a theme via the three-dot menu ("Duplicate"). The copy is named "Copy of …" and stays unpublished until you publish it.

## After: check the result

1. **Same measurement as before.** The same three pages, the same tool.
2. **Test functions.** A test order with payment, the checkout on a phone, the most important apps, forms.
3. **Watch Search Console.** Messages and redirects. Shopify's reports cover the past 30 days, so a change shows up there only gradually.
4. **Read the change list.** If the list is missing, ask for it.

On Search Central, Google recommends good Core Web Vitals for success in Search. No specific ranking follows from that.

## An example: Pedchem

[Pedchem](/projects/pedchem), a pest control store with 323 products, ran on the Flex theme. I rebuilt the store on the Enterprise theme without adding any apps, gave the product data in metafields proper types, fixed errors from Search Console and set up redirects from its data. Live mobile PageSpeed on the home page went from 57 to 82 (median of three runs), and at day 28 the field LCP fell from 3.0 to 1.5 seconds. The speed targets were agreed in advance. The build ran on a preview theme while the old theme kept selling. It was published only after written approval.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I start myself?** Yes. Cleaning up apps, shrinking images and shortening the home page often does a lot. The list is in [Slow Shopify store?](/blog/slow-shopify-store).

**Do I need a new theme?** Not always. When cleaning up is enough and when a switch is better is covered in [Shopify theme customization](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).

**Can anyone guarantee the store sells more afterwards?** No. Load time, errors and usability can be improved. Revenue also depends on range, prices and advertising.
