Your Shopify theme almost fits: a button sits in the wrong place, a detail is missing on the product page, or a feature only exists as a paid app. You can solve a lot yourself in the editor. For the rest you can hire someone to customize your Shopify theme without putting your live store at risk.

## Check first: what you can do in the editor

Under **Online Store › Themes › Customize** you change these without code:

- **Colors and fonts** for the whole store.
- **Sections**: add, move and hide them.
- **Text, images and buttons** in every section.
- **Your own templates**, for example a second product template for a group of products.
- **Menus** under Content › Menus.
- **Dynamic sources**: connect a section's fields to metafields, so each product shows its own details.

Test in a **copy of your theme** (Themes › Actions › Duplicate). It is unpublished, customers do not see it. If that is enough, you do not need anyone.

## When a developer is worth it

The editor only shows settings the theme developer planned for. Anything else means changing code in the theme. Typical cases:

- **A missing feature**, for example an "Add to cart" button on related products.
- **A different layout**, such as the product page on a phone.
- **Structured product data**: tables, ingredients, dimensions or downloads from metafields, see [Shopify metafields and metaobjects](/blog/shopify-metafelder-metaobjects).
- **Replacing an app**, when what it does also works in the theme, with no monthly fee and no third-party code.
- **Bugs in the theme** after app changes or old customizations.
- **Navigation**, for example breadcrumbs for nested collections.

## The briefing: three questions

1. **What bothers you, and where exactly?** A screenshot with a marking is enough. Name the page and the device, for example "product page on a phone".
2. **What should it look like when it is done?** An example from another store, a sketch or a sentence like "the button should be visible without scrolling".
3. **What must not change?** The rest of the design, the page addresses, running apps or campaigns.

Add the name of your theme (under Online Store › Themes) and the apps that show something in the store.

## What a good offer for theme customization contains

- **Every change in one sentence**, including what does not get built.
- **Work in an unpublished copy.** Your live store keeps selling unchanged.
- **Preview and approval.** You check on phone and desktop, and nothing is published until you confirm.
- **Sections with settings wherever possible.** Then you maintain texts and images yourself in the editor.
- **A list of changed files.** When you update a theme from the Theme Store, Shopify carries over settings and content, but not changes to the code. Those have to be added again afterwards.
- **A clear price or a clear pricing logic.** With me it is a fixed price after the briefing. What prices depend on: [Having a Shopify store built: what the cost depends on](/blog/shopify-shop-erstellen-lassen-kosten).

## Warning signs

- **Someone asks for your password.** Developers get their own kind of access.
- **Work directly on the live theme.** Your customers see every mistake right away.
- **No list of changes.** At the next theme update nobody knows what has to be added again.
- **Hard-coded where a setting would have done.** Then you need the developer again for every text.
- **A new app for every task.** Every app can add code to the theme that slows the store down, see [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).

## Giving access safely

According to the Shopify help, access for developers runs through collaborator requests, not through your password:

- The developer sends the request through a Partner account. You see it in the settings under "Users" and review the permissions. Shopify advises granting only the ones the task needs.
- In the Security section you can generate a four-digit code that the developer has to enter with the request. A new code invalidates old ones.
- You can remove access at any time. It expires automatically if the developer has not logged in for 90 days.

## After: check the result

1. **Open the preview.** Check the pages from the briefing on phone and desktop.
2. **Click through to checkout.** That shows whether anything else broke.
3. **Try it in the editor.** Can you edit and hide the new area?
4. **File the list of changes** for the next theme update.

Only then do you approve.

## Customize or a new theme?

When the wish list gets long, the old theme may no longer be the right foundation. A newer theme already does a lot of what the old one needs apps and workarounds for. More on [Shopify redesign and theme change](/services/shopify-redesign).
