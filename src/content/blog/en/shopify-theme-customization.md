Your Shopify theme fits almost, but only almost? A button sits in the wrong place, a detail is missing on the product page, or a feature only exists as a paid app. This article helps you decide: what you can solve yourself in the editor, when to have the theme customized, what belongs in the briefing and the offer, and how to hand over the work without putting your live store at risk.

## Check first: what you can do in the editor

Under **Online Store › Themes › Customize** there is more than many people think:

- **Colors and fonts** for the whole store, in the theme settings.
- **Adding, moving and hiding sections**, on the home page and on every other page.
- **Text, images and buttons** in every section.
- **Your own templates**, for example a second product template for a certain group of products.
- **Menus** under Content › Menus, independent of the theme.
- **Dynamic sources**: connecting a section's fields to metafields, so each product shows its own details.

Try changes in a **copy of your theme** (Themes › Actions › Duplicate). The copy is unpublished, your customers do not see it. If that solves what you want, you do not need anyone.

## When a developer is worth it

The editor only shows the settings the theme developer planned for. Anything beyond that means changing code in the theme. Typical cases:

- **A feature the theme does not have**, for example an "Add to cart" button right on related products.
- **A layout the section does not allow**, such as a different arrangement of the product page on a phone.
- **Product data that should be shown in a structured way**: tables, ingredients, dimensions or downloads from metafields. How to set up that data is in [Shopify metafields and metaobjects](/blog/shopify-metafelder-metaobjects).
- **Replacing an app**, when what it does also works cleanly in the theme and then costs no monthly fee and no third-party code.
- **Bugs in the theme** that show up after app changes or old customizations.
- **Navigation and orientation**, for example breadcrumbs for nested collections.

## The briefing: three questions

A good briefing is short. It answers three questions:

1. **What bothers you, and where exactly?** A screenshot with a marking says more than a long text. Name the page and the device: "product page on a phone" is a different job from "home page on desktop".
2. **What should it look like when it is done?** An example from another store, a sketch or a sentence like "the button should be visible without scrolling".
3. **What must not change?** The rest of the design, the page addresses, running apps or campaigns.

Add the name of your theme (shown under Online Store › Themes) and the apps that show something in the store. A developer needs nothing more for a first estimate.

## What a good offer for theme customization contains

- **Every change in one sentence.** What gets built, and what explicitly does not.
- **Work in an unpublished copy.** Your live store keeps selling unchanged.
- **A preview to check**, on phone and desktop, before anything goes live.
- **Your approval.** Nothing is published until you confirm.
- **Sections with settings wherever possible.** Then you maintain texts and images yourself in the editor afterwards.
- **A list of changed files.** Important for later theme updates, more on that in a moment.
- **A clear price or a clear pricing logic.** With me it is a fixed price after the briefing; what prices generally depend on is in [Having a Shopify store built: what the cost depends on](/blog/shopify-shop-erstellen-lassen-kosten).

**Theme updates belong in the conversation.** When you update a theme from the Theme Store, Shopify carries over your settings and content, but not changes to the code. Customized code has to be added again afterwards. A good offer says how the changes are documented so that this takes a manageable amount of time.

## Warning signs

- **Someone asks for your password.** Developers have their own kind of access, see below.
- **Work directly on the live theme.** Any mistake is then visible to your customers right away.
- **No list of changes.** Then at the next theme update nobody knows what has to be added again.
- **The developer again for every small thing.** If you cannot even change a text yourself after the customization, it was hard-coded where a setting would have done.
- **A new app for every task.** Sometimes an app is the right answer. But every extra app can add code to the theme that slows the store down. More in [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).

## Giving access safely

You do not have to hand over your password. According to the Shopify help, access for developers runs through collaborator requests:

- The developer sends a request through a Partner account. You see it in the settings under "Users" and review the permissions asked for. Shopify advises granting only the permissions the task needs.
- In addition you can generate a four-digit code there, in the Security section; the developer has to enter it with their request. A new code invalidates old ones.
- You can remove access at any time. It also expires automatically if the developer has not logged in for 90 days.

Clarify beforehand which permissions the theme work really needs and when access will be withdrawn.

## After: check the result

1. **Open the preview on your phone.** Check exactly the pages from your briefing, on phone and desktop.
2. **Click through to checkout.** Add a product to the cart and go on to checkout. That shows whether the change broke anything else.
3. **Try it in the editor.** Can you edit the new area yourself, hide it, fill it with a different text?
4. **File the list of changes.** You will need it at the next theme update or when someone else works on the store later.

Only then do you approve.

## Customize or a new theme?

When the wish list gets long, it is worth asking whether the old theme is still the right foundation. A newer theme already does a lot of what the old one needs apps and workarounds for. Whether customizing or switching gets you further is usually clear quickly once someone has looked at the theme from the inside. ## Frequently asked questions

**Freelancer or agency for a theme customization?** For individual changes one person who writes Liquid is often enough. Which questions to ask any provider is in [Shopify freelancer or agency](/blog/shopify-freelancer-oder-agentur). I am a freelancer myself, which you should know while reading.

**Can I keep editing my theme in the editor afterwards?** Yes, if the customizations are built as sections and settings. That is how I build them.

**Does my store keep selling while the work is done?** Yes. The work happens in an unpublished copy, which only goes live after your approval.

**What happens on a theme update?** Settings and content stay, code changes have to be added again. With clean documentation that takes a manageable amount of time.

**What does a customization cost?** That depends on what should change. After a short briefing you get a fixed price before I start.
