A "Shopify SEO" quote is often a long list: sitemap, canonical tags, SSL, structured data. Shopify already ships a large part of that. Before hiring someone for Shopify SEO, look at what is actually left to you. If your store does not show up on Google at all, start with [Shopify store not found on Google](/blog/shopify-shop-bei-google-nicht-gefunden).

## What Shopify does by itself

According to the Shopify Help Center, these are built in:

- **`sitemap.xml`** is generated automatically and updated for new products, collections, pages, images or posts.
- **`robots.txt`** excludes things like the cart, checkout and customer accounts.
- **Canonical tags** keep duplicate content out of search results.
- **SSL certificates** are on by default.
- **Title tags** are generated by the theme. If the page title is empty, it uses the store name and the title of the content.
- **Structured data for products** comes from the theme, according to Shopify.

If an offer lists exactly these points as work, ask what is left to do. Checking is still worthwhile: the Help Center says missing titles or H1 headings usually come from custom code or an app. You can test structured data with Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results) without verifying your store.

Valid structured data only makes a page **eligible** for special display. Whether price, availability or reviews appear is Google's decision.

## What stays with you

**Title and description per page.** Products, collections, pages and blog posts have a *Search engine listing preview* area with *Edit website SEO*. The home page is under *Online Store → Preferences*. According to Google, each title should fit its page and be clear and concise. Identical descriptions on every page do not help. For large catalogs, Google accepts programmatically generated descriptions.

**Your own copy.** Shopify advises against reusing the manufacturer's description because it may already be on other websites. Google says this is not a violation, but it is inefficient. For the products and collections that matter, write copy that answers your customers' questions.

**Alt text.** On a product, click the image and choose *Edit alt text*. Google describes alt text as a short text explaining how the image relates to your content.

**Structure and internal links.** Shopify says every page should be linked in your menus and, where possible, no more than three clicks from the home page. Link text describes the destination rather than saying "click here". More: [Shopify collections and filters](/blog/shopify-kollektionen-filter).

**Redirects.** When an address changes, create a redirect under *Content → Menus → URL redirects*. According to the Help Center it works immediately, but only for addresses that already return a 404 error. More: [Shopify migration and SEO: setting up redirects](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen).

**Hiding pages on purpose.** For test pages or duplicates, products have the *Unlisted* status and the `seo.hidden` metafield. According to the Help Center both remove the page from the sitemap and search engines. A `robots.txt` block can stop crawling, Google says, but does not always prevent indexing.

## Numbers that get passed around

Character and word counts are often sold as rules. The sources are less clear-cut:

- **Title:** depending on the page, the Shopify Help Center says under 60 or up to 70 characters. Google says there is no limit, and titles are truncated by device width.
- **Description:** Shopify recommends 160 characters, and its page on collections mentions descriptions being cut off above 320. Google names no limit here either.
- **Text length:** Shopify recommends at least 250 words for products, collections and pages, and 500 for informational pages and blog posts. According to Google, length alone does not matter for ranking.
- **Meta keywords:** Google does not use the keywords meta tag. Anyone billing you for maintaining it is working on something with no effect.

Put the important part of the title first and write as much text as your customers need.

## What you are better off leaving alone

The `robots.txt` file can be changed through the `robots.txt.liquid` template. Shopify calls this an unsupported customization: support will not help, and mistakes can cost you all your traffic, according to the Help Center. Shopify describes the default file as optimal for SEO. Only change it for a specific reason.

Changes to `theme.liquid` and product templates also belong in a copy of the theme, because titles or headings can disappear in the process.

## What an SEO job can reasonably include

Among the services of SEO professionals, Google lists a review of content and structure, technical advice on things like redirects, help with content, keyword research and training. For a Shopify store, that splits like this:

- **You can usually do this yourself:** titles, descriptions, alt text, your own copy, menus. According to Google, small local businesses can probably do much of the work themselves.
- **A developer makes sense for:** broken or missing titles and headings in the theme, Search Console errors, redirects in larger numbers, load time, structured data after theme changes. On load time: [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).
- **You need someone other than a developer for:** ongoing keyword research, an editorial plan and copywriting. That is marketing work. I mainly do the technical part.

## How to spot a serious offer

Google recommends asking these questions, among others, before you hire:

1. Can you show examples of previous work?
2. Do you follow the Google Search Essentials?
3. What results do you expect, in what timeframe, and how do you measure success?
4. What experience do you have in my industry and my country?
5. How will you tell me about changes to my store?

Google also advises checking recommendations against its official documentation. For Shopify I would add: a good offer names the pages to be worked on, records baseline figures from Search Console and describes how the result will be checked. More: [Hiring someone to optimize your Shopify store](/blog/shopify-shop-optimieren-lassen).

## Warning signs

- **A guaranteed number one spot.** Nobody can guarantee that, Google writes.
- **A "special relationship" with Google** or "priority submit". Google warns about these. Google takes no money for ranking, and advertising with Google has no effect on organic results.
- **Submission to thousands of search engines** and link schemes.
- **Unsolicited emails** offering SEO.
- **Keyword stuffing.** It is against Google's spam policies.
- **Changes nobody will explain.**

## Giving access without risk

For an SEO review, Google says read access to Search Console is enough. In the Shopify admin, a developer works through a collaborator account with limited permissions, and your owner login stays with you. Details: [Switching your Shopify developer](/blog/shopify-entwickler-wechseln).

## How long it takes

There is no fixed time. According to Google, some changes take effect within hours and others take months. In general, wait a few weeks. Changed titles only appear after the next crawl, so after a few days to a few weeks. Do not rewrite everything each week, or you will not know what worked.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an SEO app?** Not automatically. Google does not evaluate or endorse third-party SEO tools, and every app is one more component in your store.

**Is submitting the sitemap to Google enough?** It helps Google find pages but says nothing about ranking. According to Google it is not strictly required.

**Can you guarantee me a ranking?** No. I can remove technical obstacles and show what was changed. The rest depends on content and competition.

**What does it cost?** That depends on how many pages are affected and what needs doing in the theme. With me, you get a fixed-price offer after the briefing.
