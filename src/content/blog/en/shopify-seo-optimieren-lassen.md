Ask for a "Shopify SEO" quote and you often get a long list: sitemap, canonical tags, SSL, structured data. Shopify already ships a large part of that. This article separates what the platform does by itself, what stays with you and how to check an SEO offer. If your store does not show up on Google at all, start with [Shopify store not found on Google](/blog/shopify-shop-bei-google-nicht-gefunden). That one is about indexing, this one is about what you should actually pay for.

## What Shopify does by itself

According to the Shopify Help Center, these are built in:

- **`sitemap.xml`** is generated automatically and updated when you add products, collections, pages, images or posts.
- **`robots.txt`** exists and excludes things like the cart, checkout and customer accounts.
- **Canonical tags** are added automatically so duplicate content does not appear in search results.
- **SSL certificates** are on by default.
- **Title tags** are generated by the theme. If you leave the page title empty, it uses your store name together with the title of the content.
- **Structured data for products** comes from the theme, according to Shopify.

If an offer lists exactly these points as work, ask what is left to do on them. Checking is still worthwhile: customized themes and apps can interfere with built-in features. The Help Center says missing titles or H1 headings usually come from custom code or an app. You can test a page's structured data with Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results), and you do not need to verify your store for that.

One thing to keep in mind: valid structured data only makes a page **eligible** for special display. Whether price, availability or reviews actually appear is Google's decision.

## What stays with you

**Title and description per page.** Products, collections, pages and blog posts have a *Search engine listing preview* area with *Edit website SEO*. The home page is under *Online Store → Preferences*. Google's advice: each title should fit its page and be clear and concise. Identical or similar descriptions on every page do not help, according to Google. For large catalogs, Google explicitly accepts programmatically generated descriptions.

**Your own copy.** Shopify advises against reusing the manufacturer's description because it may already be on other websites. Google says this is not a violation and will not cause a manual action, but it is inefficient. For you that means: on the products and collections that matter, write copy that answers your customers' questions.

**Alt text.** On a product, click the image and choose *Edit alt text*. Google describes alt text as a short, descriptive text explaining how the image relates to your content.

**Structure and internal links.** Shopify says every page should be linked in your menus and, where possible, no more than three clicks from the home page. Link text should describe the destination rather than say "click here". How collections help with this is covered in [Shopify collections and filters](/blog/shopify-kollektionen-filter).

**Redirects.** When an address changes, create a redirect under *Content → Menus → URL redirects*. According to the Help Center it works immediately, but only for addresses that already return a 404 error. More in [Shopify migration and SEO: setting up redirects](/blog/shopify-migration-weiterleitungen).

**Hiding pages on purpose.** Test pages or duplicates should stay out of search. Products have the *Unlisted* status, and there is the `seo.hidden` metafield. According to the Help Center both remove the page from the sitemap and search engines. A `robots.txt` block is not meant for this: Google says it can stop crawling but does not always prevent indexing.

## Numbers that get passed around

Character and word counts are often sold as rules. The sources are less clear-cut:

- **Title:** depending on the page, the Shopify Help Center says under 60 or up to 70 characters. Google says there is no limit, but the title gets truncated to fit the device width.
- **Description:** Shopify recommends 160 characters. Its page on collections mentions descriptions being cut off above 320. Google again says: no limit, truncated by device width.
- **Text length:** Shopify recommends at least 250 words for products, collections and pages, and 500 for informational pages and blog posts. Google writes that length alone does not matter for ranking and that there is no magic word count.
- **Meta keywords:** Google does not use the keywords meta tag. Anyone billing you for maintaining it is working on something with no effect.

In practice: keep titles short enough that the important part comes first, and write as much text as the page needs for your customers.

## What you are better off leaving alone

The `robots.txt` file can be changed through the `robots.txt.liquid` template. Shopify calls this an unsupported customization, its support will not help with it, and incorrect use can cost you all your traffic, according to the Help Center. Shopify describes the default file as optimal for SEO. Only change it if someone can name a specific reason.

The same goes for changes to `theme.liquid` or product templates if titles or headings could disappear in the process. That kind of work belongs in a copy of the theme.

## What an SEO job can reasonably include

Among the services SEO professionals offer, Google lists a review of your site's content and structure, technical advice on things like redirects, error pages and JavaScript, help with content, keyword research and training. For a Shopify store, that splits roughly like this:

- **You can usually do this yourself:** titles, descriptions, alt text, your own copy, menus. Google writes that small local businesses can probably do much of the work themselves.
- **A developer makes sense for:** broken or missing titles and headings in the theme, Search Console errors, redirects in larger numbers, load time, structured data after theme changes. On load time: [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).
- **You need someone other than a developer for:** ongoing keyword research, an editorial plan and copywriting. That is marketing work. I mainly do the technical part and I say so.

## How to spot a serious offer

Google recommends asking these questions, among others, before you hire:

1. Can you show examples of previous work?
2. Do you follow the Google Search Essentials?
3. What results do you expect, in what timeframe, and how do you measure success?
4. What experience do you have in my industry and my country?
5. How will you tell me about changes to my store?

Google also advises checking recommendations against its official documentation. For a Shopify store I would add: a good offer names the pages to be worked on, records baseline figures from Search Console and describes how the result will be checked. What this looks like for an optimization job as a whole is covered in [Hiring someone to optimize your Shopify store](/blog/shopify-shop-optimieren-lassen).

## Warning signs

- **A guaranteed number one spot.** Nobody can guarantee that, Google writes. Anyone promising it is, according to Google, not the right choice.
- **A "special relationship" with Google** or "priority submit". Google warns about these explicitly. Google also takes no money to include or rank sites, and advertising with Google has no effect on organic results.
- **Submission to thousands of search engines** and link schemes.
- **Unsolicited emails** offering SEO.
- **Keyword stuffing.** It is against Google's spam policies.
- **Changes nobody will explain.** Anyone working on your store should be able to explain every change.

## Giving access without risk

For an SEO review, Google says read access to Search Console is enough; write access is not needed. In the Shopify admin, a developer works through a collaborator account with limited permissions, and your owner login stays with you. Details are in [Switching your Shopify developer](/blog/shopify-entwickler-wechseln).

## How long it takes

There is no fixed time. Google writes that some changes take effect within hours and others take months, and that in general you should wait a few weeks. Changed titles only appear once Google has recrawled the page, which can take a few days to a few weeks. So do not rewrite everything each week, or you will not know what worked.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an SEO app?** Not automatically. Google does not evaluate or endorse third-party SEO tools, and every app is one more component in your store. Shopify is testing AI suggestions for titles and descriptions with Grow SEO, according to the Help Center so far in early access for some merchants and in English only.

**Is submitting the sitemap to Google enough?** It helps Google find pages but says nothing about ranking. According to Google it is not even strictly required, since most pages are found automatically.

**Can you guarantee me a ranking?** No. I can remove technical obstacles and show what was changed. Where your store lands after that also depends on content and competition.

**What does it cost?** That depends on how many pages are affected and what needs doing in the theme. With me, you get a fixed-price offer after the briefing.
