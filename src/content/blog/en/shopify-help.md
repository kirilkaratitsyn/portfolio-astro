Shopify help comes from four places. Which one is responsible depends on where the problem sits. If you know that, you save days of going back and forth.

## Four places, four responsibilities

**1. The Shopify Help Center and Shopify Support.** Responsible for the platform: plan and billing, payments through Shopify Payments, domains, the admin, orders, shipping settings. Here you learn *where* something is in the admin and *how* a feature is meant to work.

**2. The app maker.** A third-party app is supported by its vendor. If the review app shows stars wrongly or the subscription tool does not create an order, write to the app support first. You find the contact in the Shopify App Store.

**3. The theme maker.** For a theme from the Theme Store, its maker answers questions about features and settings, for example about a certain section.

**4. A developer.** Responsible for everything *custom*: customized theme code, the interplay of several apps, errors after a change, features that do not exist as such.

## How to tell who is responsible

Two questions help:

- **Does it also happen in a fresh store without customizations?** If yes, it is the platform or the app. If no, it is your theme or a customization.
- **Did something change before?** A new app, a theme update, a new code snippet: the moment from which something stopped working is often the best clue.

## Common problems and where to take them

- **Setting not found or feature not understood:** Help Center.
- **A payment or payout does not work:** Shopify Support.
- **An app shows something wrong or does not create data:** app support; if two apps clash, a developer as well.
- **The store suddenly got slow:** usually apps or scripts in the theme: [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).
- **A button, a spacing or a section looks wrong on the phone:** check in the theme editor first, otherwise a developer: [Shopify theme customization](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).
- **A customization is missing after a theme update:** a developer, ideally one who knows the customization.
- **Moving from another system to Shopify:** a developer: [Moving from WooCommerce to Shopify](/blog/woocommerce-to-shopify).
- **A feature your theme does not have:** a developer, when no app covers it cleanly.

## What to give the developer

Note down five details:

1. **Where:** the exact page, ideally with its address.
2. **What you expected and what happens instead.**
3. **On which device and in which browser.**
4. **Since when**, and what you changed before.
5. **A screenshot or a short screen recording.**

## One-off help or ongoing support?

Some stores only need a quick fix now and then. Others change something all the time: new campaigns, new markets, new features. Then a fixed technical contact who knows the store is worth it.

More in [Shopify maintenance and support](/blog/shopify-wartung-betreuung). One ongoing example from my work is [Elanora Skin](/projects/elanora-skin), since late 2025.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Shopify Support help with code problems in the theme?** As a rule, no. Shopify supports the platform, not your custom changes.

**Do I need someone who speaks German?** It helps when the description and follow-up questions work without translation. I work in German and English.

**Can I describe a bug myself without being technical?** Yes. The five details above are enough.

**Do you work in the live store?** No. Changes are made in an unpublished copy of the theme and go live only after your approval.
