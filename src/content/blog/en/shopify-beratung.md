"Shopify consulting" can mean very different things: a free intro call, a written review of your store, a paid session on one specific question, or a fixed contact person over months. If you know beforehand what you need, you get a lot more out of the conversation. This article sorts the formats, says when you do not need consulting at all, and shows how to prepare.

## When consulting is worth it

Consulting pays off mostly **before a decision** that is expensive to undo later:

- **Changing platforms.** You are thinking about moving from WooCommerce, Wix, Shopware or Magento to Shopify and want to know what comes along and what has to be rebuilt. More in [Moving from WooCommerce to Shopify](/blog/woocommerce-to-shopify).
- **Relaunch or theme change.** Is a new theme worth it, or is it enough to adjust the current one? See [Hiring someone for a Shopify relaunch](/blog/shopify-relaunch-theme-wechsel).
- **New markets or trade customers.** Several countries, languages and currencies, or an area for wholesale customers with their own prices, change how the store is built. See [Shopify Markets](/blog/shopify-markets-mehrere-laender) and [Shopify B2B](/blog/shopify-b2b-wholesale).
- **App or customization.** For many wishes there is an app, for some a change in the theme is cleaner, for a few a custom app. See [Having a Shopify app built](/blog/shopify-app-entwickeln-lassen).
- **Quotes you cannot compare.** You have two or three quotes and each one describes something different. A second opinion on the scope often saves more than it costs.
- **A problem that has been there for weeks.** The store is slow, a feature does not work, and nobody feels responsible.

## When you do not need consulting

Not every question needs a consultant:

- **You cannot find a setting or do not understand a feature:** that is what the Shopify Help Center is for; for payments, plan or domain, Shopify Support.
- **An app misbehaves:** write to the app developer first.

Which party handles what is covered in detail in [Shopify help: when the Help Center is enough](/blog/shopify-help). Consulting starts to make sense once it is about **your** store: your theme, your apps, your plans.

## Four kinds of consulting

**1. Intro call.** Usually short and free. It clarifies what this is about, whether the person is a fit and what the next steps would be. Do not expect a finished concept from it: without looking into the store, nobody can seriously say how much work something is.

**2. Shop-Check or review.** Someone looks at your store and writes down what stands out. Some things are visible from the outside, such as load time, embedded apps and page structure. Anything in the admin, such as settings or theme code, needs access.

**3. Paid consulting on a specific question.** For example: which data comes along in a migration? How do we handle trade prices? The result should be in writing, so you can hand it to another developer later.

**4. Ongoing support.** A fixed contact person who knows the store and does not start from zero every time. Worth it for stores that change things all the time. More in [Shopify maintenance and support](/blog/shopify-wartung-betreuung).

## Who does the consulting

Besides Shopify Support, which handles the platform itself, agencies and freelancers offer consulting. One overview is the **Shopify Partner Directory**: according to the Shopify Help Center it lists established agency partners that you can contact directly. Partners are admitted from a certain tier in the Partner Program. So someone who is not listed is not necessarily worse; they just have not reached that tier or are not an agency. What speaks for a freelancer and what for an agency is in [Shopify freelancer or agency](/blog/shopify-freelancer-oder-agentur).

## How to prepare for the conversation

The better you prepare, the less time goes into basics. Write down beforehand:

1. **Your store address**, so the person can look around first.
2. **Your goal in one sentence.** Not "the store should get better", but for example "trade customers should be able to reorder without calling".
3. **What you have already tried**, and with what result.
4. **The apps you use**, and which of them you really need.
5. **A deadline, if there is one**, such as a trade fair, a season or the cancellation date of the old system.
6. **A sense of the scale.** It helps to say whether you have a small fix or a bigger rebuild in mind.
7. **Who decides.** If that person joins the call, you save a second one.

## What you should have in hand at the end

After good consulting you have more than a good feeling:

- a **written summary** of the key points,
- **next steps** in a clear order,
- a split between what **you can do yourself** and what you need help with,
- a note on what the consulting **could not** settle, such as tax or legal questions,
- if you want a quote: a described scope that you can compare with other quotes.

## Questions you should ask

- **Can you show examples of similar stores?**
- **Who will actually work on my store**, you or someone else?
- **How do you get access?** The usual way is a collaborator account with limited permissions. Your owner login stays with you. Details in [Switching your Shopify developer](/blog/shopify-entwickler-wechseln).
- **Do you earn anything from apps or themes you recommend?** A commission is not forbidden, but you should know about it.
- **What happens if it turns out along the way that things work differently than planned?**

## Warning signs

- **Advice without having seen the store.** General tips are in the Help Center too.
- **A complete rebuild right away**, without explaining why changes to the current store are not enough.
- **A request for your owner password.** That is what collaborator accounts are for.
- **Guarantees on revenue or rankings.** Nobody can seriously promise what your customers will do or where Google will place you.
- **No written result.** What was only said out loud is forgotten two weeks later.
