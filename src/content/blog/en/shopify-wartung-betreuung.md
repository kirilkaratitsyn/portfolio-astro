"You don't have to maintain anything on Shopify" is half true. Shopify is hosted: the platform handles servers, platform updates and the checkout. Maintenance and support for the theme, apps, access and content stay with you.

## What Shopify takes off your hands

You do not install platform updates or run your own server (comparison: [WooCommerce security and plugin updates](/blog/woocommerce-sicherheit-plugin-updates)). On its [security page](https://www.shopify.com/security) Shopify states, among other things, certification to PCI DSS Level 1 and SOC 2 Type II and SOC 3 reports.

Shopify says some data protection steps remain yours, and for legal questions it points to a lawyer. I cover neither here.

## What stays with you

### 1. Access

According to the Shopify Help Center:

- Everyone working in the store has their own staff account with fitting permissions.
- The owner's login details are not shared, not even with staff.
- For Shopify Payments, two-step authentication on your account is required.
- If an account is compromised, contact Shopify Support immediately and secure the account.

Remove anyone who no longer works on the store: staff, agency, former freelancers.

### 2. Apps

Every installed app is third-party code. In the admin, an app's "Activity and permissions" section shows which areas of your store it can view or edit. Check whether you still need the app and whether the access fits. More apps often make the store slower, see [Slow Shopify store? 7 causes](/blog/slow-shopify-store).

### 3. The theme

Shopify does not move new theme versions into your live theme on its own. According to the Help Center, you add the new version as a draft from the notification. According to Shopify:

- Customizations from the theme editor are carried over, including added, reordered or hidden sections.
- Code edits are carried over only if they do not conflict with the update. Otherwise the admin reports that they are missing, and you copy them over yourself.
- Shopify recommends always saving a copy of customized code before updating.

This is where stores without support most often lose customizations: one click on "update", and the special solution from a year ago is gone. Do not apply updates to a customized theme on your own. When code is needed: [Shopify theme customization](/blog/shopify-theme-customization).

### 4. Copies and exports

According to the Help Center, products can be exported to CSV: the current page, all products, selected ones or those matching a search. Take a regular copy of your key data. The help pages I checked say nothing about backups of themes and store data. Check in your account and apps what is backed up.

### 5. Content and tests

No update, new app or new code belongs in the live store untested. After every change, run through it on a smartphone: home page, a product, cart, checkout up to the payment choice.

## A simple rhythm

A recommendation from practice, not a Shopify requirement:

- **Monthly** (15 minutes): a test order or a run to the checkout on a smartphone, read the Shopify notifications, check unusual orders.
- **Quarterly:** go through staff and apps, remove unused apps, check the theme version, copy key data.
- **When changing things:** a theme update or larger customization only in a copy, then test, then publish.

## When ongoing support is worth it

If you rarely change anything, the rhythm above is enough. For problems there are the Help Center and support ([Shopify help](/blog/shopify-help)). Fixed support is more worthwhile if

- your theme has a lot of code that gets in the way of updates,
- promotions, markets or features keep being added,
- nobody on the team has time for apps and access,
- a checkout outage would noticeably cost revenue.

An example: I have worked on the [Elanora Skin](/projects/elanora-skin) store on an ongoing basis since late 2025, not as an off-the-shelf maintenance package. I can only say what makes sense for you once I have seen your theme, apps and planned changes.

## What to settle before a support agreement

Ask a freelancer or an agency alike:

1. **What exactly is included?** Theme updates, app review, small changes, bug fixing: in writing, not "everything technical".
2. **How does a change work?** In a copy of the theme, published only after your approval?
3. **Which access is needed?** A staff or collaborator access with limited rights is usual, not your owner login. It ends with the agreement.
4. **How fast do they reply?** Response time for outages, availability, a stand-in.
5. **How is it billed?** A fixed price after a briefing, a block of hours or hourly, and what applies if more effort is needed?
6. **Who owns what at the end?** Code, documentation and access should be with you when the agreement ends.

Individual or team: [Shopify freelancer or agency](/blog/shopify-freelancer-oder-agentur).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to install security updates on Shopify?** Not for the platform. For your theme, apps and access, yes.

**Will I lose customizations in a theme update?** Not those from the theme editor, which the Help Center says are carried over. Code edits can be lost. Save a copy first.

**Do I need a maintenance contract?** Not necessarily. It pays off with frequent changes or customized code.

**What does support cost?** It depends on the scope. I do not name amounts here.
