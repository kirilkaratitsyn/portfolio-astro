Freelancer or agency for your Shopify store? I am a freelancer myself, so I am not neutral. I still say when an agency is the better choice.

## The real difference

It is not "big versus small" but three questions: Who works on your store? How much runs at the same time? What happens when someone drops out?

**Freelancer:** one person. You talk directly to whoever writes the code. But one person does not deliver design, development, photos and advertising all at once, and during holiday or illness the project stops.

**Agency:** a team, usually with project management, design, development and often marketing. That brings breadth, capacity and cover. In return there are more interfaces, your contact does not always do the work, and the structure is part of the price.

## When a freelancer fits

- **The scope is clear:** a theme customization, a redesign, a move, a faster store, a single feature.
- **You want a direct line** to the person building your store.
- **You want ongoing support** from someone who knows the store.
- **Your store is small to mid-sized** and you have no team to steer an agency project.

## When an agency fits better

- **You need a lot from one source at the same time:** design, development, photos and video, copy, advertising.
- **The project is big and has a fixed date**, such as a relaunch before a campaign with several parallel workstreams.
- **The store depends on many systems** such as ERP and inventory that need several specialists.
- **You need guaranteed cover**, for example because your company chooses service providers by fixed rules.

If several of these apply, do not start with a freelancer alone.

## The risks with a freelancer and how to keep them small

1. **Dropping out.** The store sits on your Shopify account, and you can withdraw the developer's access at any time. Then someone else can take over.
2. **Knowledge in one head.** Ask whether changes are recorded in a traceable way.
3. **Capacity.** Ask how many projects run in parallel and when a start is realistic.
4. **Proof.** Ask for examples with a link to a live store.

## Six questions to ask every provider

1. **Who exactly works on my store?**
2. **Can you show me a comparable store that is live?**
3. **Does the store run on my account, and is everything mine in the end?**
4. **What is included in the fixed price, and what is not?** (More in [What the cost depends on](/blog/shopify-shop-erstellen-lassen-kosten).)
5. **What happens after go-live** and if my contact is unreachable?
6. **Do you work in an unpublished copy,** so that my live store keeps selling until I approve?

On the offer for an optimization job: [Optimizing your Shopify store](/blog/shopify-shop-optimieren-lassen).

## Mixed setups are normal

Development and marketing do not have to sit with the same provider. A common split is a freelancer as the fixed technical contact and an agency or a specialist for advertising. One person has to really know the store.

## An example from my work

I have looked after [Elanora Skin](/projects/elanora-skin), a skincare brand from Breda, continuously since late 2025. That covers work on the theme, custom checkout extensions and a skincare quiz. That suits a freelancer, because the scope keeps growing and knowing the store counts. A relaunch with a photo shoot, a campaign and an ERP connection by a fixed date would be a case for a team.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are freelancers cheaper than agencies?** Not as a rule. An agency also finances project management, sales and a team. Which is cheaper depends on the scope and the provider. Only compare quotes that describe the same thing.

**Can a freelancer look after a large store?** Yes, if the work runs in stages. If everything is due at once by a fixed date, capacity counts.

**What happens if the freelancer drops out?** If the store sits on your account and the changes are traceable, another developer can take over.

**Is a partner status a sign of quality?** It is a hint, but it does not replace proof such as comparable stores that are live.
