You want to have your Shopify store rebuilt, adapted or looked after, and you wonder: freelancer or agency? First an honest note: I am a freelancer myself, so I am not neutral. I still try to say when an agency is the better choice. A wrong decision costs you more in the end than any price difference between two quotes.

## The real difference

It is not "big versus small". It comes down to three questions: Who actually works on your store? How much of it at the same time? And what happens when someone drops out?

**Freelancer:** one person you get to know. You talk directly to whoever writes the code, with no handover between sales, project management and development. The limits are capacity and breadth: one person does not deliver design, development, photos and advertising at top level all at once, and during a holiday or illness the project stands still.

**Agency:** a team with roles, usually project management, design, development and often marketing. The strengths are breadth, capacity and cover. The limits: more interfaces, the contact is not always the one doing the work, and the structure (project management, sales, office) is part of the price.

## When a freelancer fits

- **The scope is clear:** a theme customization, a redesign, a move, a faster store, a single feature.
- **You want a direct line** to the person building your store, and short decision paths.
- **You are looking for ongoing support** from someone who knows the store and does not start from zero with every request.
- **Your store is small to mid-sized** and you have no team to steer an agency project.

## When an agency fits better

- **You need a lot from one source and at the same time:** design, development, photos and video, copy, advertising.
- **The project is big and has a fixed date**, such as a relaunch before a campaign where several workstreams must run in parallel. Then capacity counts.
- **The store depends on many systems** such as ERP, inventory and further connections that need several specialists.
- **You need guaranteed cover** and a contract partner with a team behind them, for example because your company chooses service providers by fixed rules.

If several of these apply, you should not start with a freelancer alone.

## The risks with a freelancer and how to keep them small

1. **Dropping out.** Make sure the store sits on your own Shopify account and the developer only has access that you can withdraw at any time. Then someone else can take over in an emergency.
2. **Knowledge in one head.** Ask whether changes are recorded in a traceable way, so another developer can pick up later.
3. **Capacity.** Ask how many projects run in parallel and when a start is realistic.
4. **Proof.** Ask for real examples, ideally with a link to a store that is live.

The same questions apply to agencies. They are not distrust, they are a normal part of choosing.

## Six questions to ask every provider

1. **Who exactly works on my store?**
2. **Can you show me a comparable store that is live?**
3. **Does the store run on my account, and is everything mine in the end?**
4. **What is included in the fixed price, and what is not?** (More in [What the cost depends on](/blog/shopify-shop-erstellen-lassen-kosten).)
5. **What happens after go-live?** And what if my contact is unreachable?
6. **Do you work in an unpublished copy,** so that my live store keeps selling until I approve?

The answers say more than any self-presentation on a website. What belongs in the offer for an optimization job in particular is covered in [Hiring Someone to Optimize Your Shopify Store](/blog/shopify-shop-optimieren-lassen).

## Mixed setups are normal

Development and marketing do not have to sit with the same provider. A common split: a freelancer as the fixed technical contact for the store, an agency or a specialist for advertising and campaigns. What matters is that one person really knows the store and the agreements hold together.

## An example from my work

I have looked after [Elanora Skin](/projects/elanora-skin), a skincare brand from Breda, on an ongoing basis since late 2025. That covers tested offers, gift logic in the cart and the storefront logic for fifteen markets in two languages. That suits a freelancer, because the scope keeps growing and the advantage lies in knowing the store. A relaunch with a photo shoot, a campaign and an ERP connection by a fixed date would be a case for a team instead.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are freelancers cheaper than agencies?** That cannot be answered in general. An agency also finances project management, sales and a team, while a freelancer has less structure behind them. Whether that ends up cheaper depends on the scope and the provider. Only compare quotes that describe the same thing.

**Can a freelancer look after a large store?** Yes, if the work is plannable and runs in stages. If everything has to be finished at once by a fixed date, capacity counts.

**What happens if the freelancer drops out?** If the store sits on your account and the changes are traceable, another developer can take over. Clarify that before the start, not after.

**Is a partner status a sign of quality?** It can be a hint, but it does not replace proof. What counts is comparable stores that are live and a clear way of working together.
